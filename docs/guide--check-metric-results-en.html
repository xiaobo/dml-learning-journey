Check three ways a metric can go wrong

On this page
  • Year-to-date margin is 25%
  • February keeps January as the comparison period
  • A duplicate lookup key can turn 30 into 60

Use the examples below to check a cumulative ratio, a comparison period, and a lookup key. Each example gives you a number or a diagnostic to look for.

Year-to-date margin is 25% #

North records 100 in revenue and 60 in cost in January, then 300 and 240 in February. The monthly margins are 40% and 20%. Their arithmetic mean is 30%. The year-to-date margin is (400 − 300) ÷ 400 = 25%.

The model adds up revenue and cost for the year to date. It subtracts total cost from total revenue, then divides by total revenue. Open the cumulative margin guide to compare the monthly and year-to-date results and inspect the model. The guide records DuckDB execution and SQL Server compilation.

February keeps January as the comparison period #

The sales query displays February. North has 30 in February sales and 20 in January sales, so growth is 50%. A SQL query that filters out January before applying LAG has no January row to compare. The model uses {sales: pp} to read the previous month in the February report. Try both month selections in the time lesson, then check the previous-sales column with the DuckDB sales exercise.

Use explicit month bounds when the report has a fixed range. See how comparison periods affect which rows appear.

A duplicate lookup key can turn 30 into 60 #

Two North sales add up to 30. If the region directory has two North rows, each sale matches twice and the joined sum becomes 60. The lookup lesson lets you see the extra rows. The DuckDB compound-key exercise takes you through a successful validation, a deliberate duplicate, and a repair. In the failure report, look for VALIDATION_ERROR and the offending key under data.issues.

Use --validate for a validation report or --with-validation with query execution to stop when a checked key fails. Compare the called measure against independent SQL on the same data to check the business result. Follow the compile, validate, and compare guide.
