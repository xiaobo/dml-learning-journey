Why you cannot average monthly gross margins

Calculate cumulative gross margin by adding revenue and cost over the period, then dividing cumulative gross profit by cumulative revenue. A simple average gives every month the same weight, even when revenues differ.

A small example

The shared profitability data contains these North sales totals:

Period Revenue Cost Gross margin
January 2025 100 60 40%
February 2025 300 240 20%
January–February 400 300 25%

The cumulative result is (400 − 300) / 400 = 25%. Averaging 40% and 20% gives 30%, which answers a different question: the unweighted average of monthly ratios. The two methods agree in some cases, such as equal monthly revenue, but cannot be substituted generally.

See the calculation change

Open the metric theatre, select North, and choose the fourth scene. Switch from the monthly context to year to date. Revenue changes from 300 to 400, cost from 240 to 300, and the margin from 20% to 25%.

The checked model accumulates the atomic revenue and cost measures before dividing. It does not sum the monthly margins:

Inspect the complete DML model 
FACT_PROFITABILITY:
  type: dataset
  table: TABLE_PROFITABILITY
  dimension: [id]
  columns:
    id:
    region:
    dt_month:
    revenue_amount:
    cost_amount:
  measures:
    net_revenue: SUM({revenue_amount})
    sales_cost: SUM({cost_amount})

gross_profit:
  type: measure
  formula: '{net_revenue} - {sales_cost}'

gross_margin:
  type: measure
  formula: 'DIVIDE({gross_profit}, {net_revenue})'

gross_margin_ytd:
  type: measure
  formula: 'DIVIDE({net_revenue: ytd} - {sales_cost: ytd}, {net_revenue: ytd})'

gross_margin_prior_ytd:
  type: measure
  formula: 'DIVIDE({net_revenue: {ytd, ya}} - {sales_cost: {ytd, ya}}, {net_revenue: {ytd, ya}})'

margin_change_pp:
  type: measure
  formula: '100 * ({gross_margin_ytd} - {gross_margin_prior_ytd})'

A difference in percentage points

North's prior-year cumulative margin is 20%. Moving from 20% to 25% is an increase of 5 percentage points. Relative growth is (25% / 20%) − 1 = 25%; label these calculations separately.
