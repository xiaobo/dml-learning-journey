Why a region filter changes the sales-share denominator

On this page
  • Keep the question fixed
  • Try both meanings
  • Choose the business meaning first

A sales share needs both a part and a whole. Removing the region grouping from the denominator does not necessarily remove the region filter. Decide whether the whole means the selected region or all regions.

Keep the question fixed #

In February, North has sales of 30 and South has 70. A North-only report still shows North when you change the denominator:

Denominator calculation Whole North share
Remove region grouping, keep its filter 30 100%
Remove region grouping and its related filter 100 30%

In DMX, d means dimension and f means filter. The reference {sales: {-d: region}} removes region grouping while keeping its related filter. The reference {sales: {-df: region}} also removes that related filter. These settings apply to this reference; they do not edit the original measure, source key or report GROUP BY.

Try both meanings #

Open the denominator exercise. Keep North selected, then switch from -d to -df. The denominator changes from 30 to 100; the displayed share changes from 100% to 30%. February remains fixed in both calculations. If no related region filter exists, the two settings give the same denominator in this example.

Choose the business meaning first #

For a share of all regions, remove the related region filter from the denominator. For a share within the selected slice, retain it. Neither choice is universally correct; the report must say which whole it uses. Other filters are not automatically removed.
